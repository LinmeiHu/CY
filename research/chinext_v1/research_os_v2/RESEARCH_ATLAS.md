# Strategy Research Atlas

This is a hypothesis-generation map, not a checklist or evidence table.

## Environment and habitat

- market regime: absolute trend direction/quality/persistence/age/alignment/
  transition; causal historical extremeness; relative index leadership;
- participation: breadth level/depth/momentum/acceleration/divergence/stability;
- volatility: level, downside/upside, vol-of-vol, contraction/expansion transition;
- dispersion/correlation: cross-sectional spread, asymmetry, co-movement;
- liquidity/risk appetite: amount, participation, limit/move tails, transitions;
- style/size/industry leadership and leadership concentration where governed.

## Daily setup and price structure

- breakout, false breakout, consolidation, compression, higher lows/lower highs,
  support/resistance, pullback, continuation, reversal;
- geometry, MA convergence, direction efficiency, volatility compression,
  volume dry-up, downside-volume decay, overhead supply, chip concentration,
  trapped holders, relative strength, trigger quality, and follow-through;
- stock-market, stock-industry, and industry-market RS level/slope/acceleration/
  persistence/age.

## Intraday and recent five-day structure

- price path, VWAP structure, buying/selling pressure proxies, intraday volatility
  contraction, volume/turnover path, objective support/resistance defense,
  breakout acceptance/rejection, and accumulation/distribution proxies;
- Day -5..Day -1 level, slope, monotonicity, endpoint change, stability, and
  reversal;
- market habitat -> daily setup -> five-day minute structure -> signal trigger;
- predictor features are separated from signal-day and post-entry attribution.

## Outcomes and strategy mechanics

- winner/loser/flat/severe-loss/false-breakout archaeology;
- right-tail formation, catastrophic-loss formation, opportunity, conversion,
  capture, giveback, exit, holding path, position management;
- execution, limits, suspensions, costs, capacity, turnover, right-tail retention,
  and failure vetoes;
- strategy habitat, state transition, interactions, combinations, and candidate
  simplicity.
