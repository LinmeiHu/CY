# ASHARE Industry-Neutral Circulating-Size Extremes Mother V1 — Visual Review Log

## Governance and scope

- Research status: development discovery; not independent confirmation.
- Mother family: the smallest and largest accepted circulating-market-value coordinate inside each PIT industry on the first completed Friday of each month.
- Signal years: 2018–2020 only.
- Chart window: 126 market sessions before and after the mother event, capped at 2021-06-30.
- Chart corpus reviewed: 220 of 220 chronological contact sheets, covering 1,975 of 1,975 individual charts.
- Outcome sheets existed only to organize visual anatomy. No 2022–2024 outcome was opened.
- The coordinate is `raw close × causally available circulating shares`. It is not total market capitalization, true free-float capitalization, enterprise value, or fundamental value.
- This log freezes the human observations before any new rule-level annual aggregation.

## What was already known before reviewing charts

- The raw mother contained 1,975 frozen events across 36 monthly decision dates, 582 symbols, and 83 PIT industries.
- Annual raw event counts were 702 in 2018, 601 in 2019, and 672 in 2020.
- Both the small and large circulating-size lanes were deliberately retained. Direction was not chosen from aggregate outcomes.
- The mother event itself was never intended to be the executable signal. The chart review was tasked with finding a later, causal stock-level confirmation or failure pattern.

## Chronological review observations

### Sheets 001–050 — early and middle 2018

- The broad weak market dominated both size lanes. Many stocks broke below the event area shortly after the monthly observation.
- Large and small circulating-size extremes both contained deep losers; the size direction did not visually separate outcomes.
- Apparent strength without subsequent retention was common: a brief lift or isolated large candle often failed and returned below the event area.
- The minority of better outcomes tended to preserve an event-area floor and later leave a compact local range, rather than merely being small or large.

### Sheets 051–070 — late 2018

- More eventual winners appeared, but many depended on the later 2019 market turn. A rule that labels the event date itself as bullish would be temporally wrong.
- Quiet, low-position structures were sometimes followed by large advances, but quietness alone also produced long stagnation or further decline.
- A later causal separation above the event-area ceiling looked more informative than the original size extreme.

### Sheets 071–110 — first half of 2019

- The early-2019 broad repair generated many winners in both size lanes.
- Static `BULL` state was insufficient: by March–April, numerous stocks were already extended or rolling over despite a favorable broad label.
- Strong-looking event candles were not reliable by themselves. Better charts typically showed continued closes above the newly cleared area; failures frequently lost the area again within a few sessions.
- A pre-event low/base was helpful in some cases but had many counterexamples and cannot be used as a standalone rule.

### Sheets 111–150 — second half of 2019 through early 2020

- Outcomes became mixed even when the market classification remained favorable or transitional.
- Winners often spent time building an event-local corridor before a delayed breakout; losers often broke the corridor floor or made a short-lived spike that failed acceptance.
- The useful distinction was path behavior after the mother event, not small-versus-large size orientation.
- Several tidy bases still failed, so visual neatness cannot be elevated into a deterministic predictor.

### Sheets 151–190 — February through August 2020

- The 2020 shock and repair produced extreme winners and extreme losers together.
- Broad repair appeared more relevant than a static market label, but it did not replace the need for stock-level acceptance.
- Many large winners left a quiet event corridor decisively and remained above its ceiling. Many severe losers either never separated or quickly returned below the same area.
- There were also false breakouts. A breakout close alone was too weak; repeated acceptance and an explicit failure exit were required by the visual evidence.

### Sheets 191–220 — August through December 2020

- Bull, transition, and bear labels all contained both winners and losers.
- Late-cycle and weak-market observations reinforced that market state alone cannot select the stock.
- Delayed corridor resolution continued to distinguish many winners from failures, but not perfectly: some winners rose immediately and some losers oscillated for months before declining.
- No visual evidence supported choosing only the small or only the large circulating-size lane.

## Cross-corpus conclusions

1. **Circulating-size extreme is a mother condition, not a direction.** Small and large industry extremes both contained profitable and losing cases.
2. **Calendar date and broad market path explain much of the raw dispersion.** Early 2018 was broadly hostile; late 2018/early 2019 and the 2020 repair supplied many winners. This does not justify a future-looking year or regime label.
3. **Static causal `BULL` is too coarse.** It admitted many late-cycle failures. A causal improvement/repair condition is economically closer to the observed transition, but must remain secondary to stock-level behavior.
4. **The most recurrent stock-level distinction is event-area resolution.** Many winners held an event-local floor, separated above the event-local ceiling, and retained that separation. Many losers broke the floor or produced only a short-lived spike.
5. **Neither low position, quietness, one breakout candle, nor visual base quality is sufficient.** Every one of these descriptions had material counterexamples.

## Contradiction ledger

- Some large winners had no tidy flat base and accelerated almost immediately.
- Some visually clean bases broke down after apparent acceptance.
- Some eventual losers remained above the event area for weeks before failing.
- Both low-position and high-position events produced winners and losers.
- Strong market repair did not save every stock; weak or transitional markets still contained isolated winners.
- Therefore the charts do not support a rich discretionary checklist or a visual classifier.

## Compressed causal candidate — frozen before annual aggregation

The visual evidence supports testing exactly one deeper version of event-area acceptance, rather than repeating generic event-high acceptance:

1. Keep the exact industry-neutral small/large circulating-size mother event.
2. Use the mother date plus the next four completed sessions only to form an objective five-session event corridor; do not trade during corridor formation.
3. From the following session through the next 15 completed sessions, reject a pre-trigger failure of two consecutive closes below the corridor floor, then require acceptance above the frozen corridor ceiling: at least three of the latest five valid closes are strictly above it, with the current close above it.
4. At that same completed close, require a causal broad-market opportunity state: either the existing `BULL` state or simultaneous 20-versus-60-session repair in both median return and positive-stock participation.
5. Buy only at the next legal open. Exit at the first legal open after two consecutive valid closes below the frozen corridor ceiling; otherwise exit at 120 market sessions. Charge 40 bps round trip.

This is a provisional, outcome-blind compression of the reviewed morphology. It is not yet a strategy and it is not evidence of profitability.
