# Material Supply Expansion: True vs False Reassertion Visual Review

## Scientific status

This is a post-hoc development anatomy of the failed
`ASHARE-MATERIAL-SUPPLY-EXPANSION-DEMAND-REASSERTION-V1` rule. It is not
independent confirmation. All 525 completed 2018-2020 parent trades were
reviewed in chronological order. No 2022-2024 outcome was read.

## Review coverage

- 59 chronological contact sheets reviewed (sheets 001-059).
- 525/525 individual trades represented.
- 385 eventual `EVENT_LOW_FAILURE` trades and 140 `TIME_EXIT_H120` trades.
- Each chart showed approximately six months before and after the supply event,
  the frozen event high/low, trigger close, entry open, exit open, turnover, and
  the parent outcome label.

## Repeated visual structure

1. **The parent entry normally has only one acceptance close.** The trigger
   close and next legal entry open are adjacent in most cases. Therefore the
   visually obvious distinction between durable acceptance and a false reclaim
   is not observable at the original entry time.
2. **Durable winners usually remain above the supply-event high after the first
   reclaim.** Many large H120 winners turn the event high into an accepted price
   floor and then continue trending.
3. **Many failures are one-day or short-lived reclaims.** They cross the event
   high, fail to remain above it, and only much later trigger the parent's very
   loose event-low exit.
4. **The parent sell rule recognizes failure too late.** Waiting for two closes
   below the event low converts loss of acceptance near the event high into a
   typical double-digit loss. The event high is the economically natural level
   whose acceptance justified entry.
5. **Some failures remain above the event high for several sessions.** A short
   acceptance delay cannot perfectly identify winners. It is a falsifiable
   timing hypothesis, not a visual certainty.

## Observations rejected as standalone rules

- Trigger distance above the event high: large winners often have a large
  margin, but direct large-margin failures exist (including roughly +9%, +12%,
  and +19% cases).
- Stock-minus-industry edge at the trigger: strong edge appears in both groups.
- Event-to-trigger delay: fast and slow triggers both win and fail.
- Event, absorption, or trigger turnover expansion: each has abundant
  counterexamples.
- Pre-event raw trend: rising, flat, and falling contexts all contain both
  outcomes.
- Causal `BULL`/`TRANSITION`/`BEAR` labels: none visually isolates the durable
  cases; the parent result was negative in each reported group.

## Compressed economic interpretation

The supply increase is only a candidate-generating event. The economically
relevant behavior is whether buyers can keep price accepted above the
pre-existing event high after the first reclaim. The chart review therefore
earns one deeper causal question: delay entry until two additional completed
sessions confirm acceptance above the same frozen event high. All other parent
conditions and the original event-low/H120 exit are preserved for the first
test so the effect of entry confirmation can be attributed cleanly.

If that entry-only test fails its frozen annual breadth/return gates, the exact
entry refinement closes. A distinct sell-timing question may then be frozen
separately because the charts also show that the existing event-low exit is
structurally late; it must not be combined with the entry change in the same
first test.

## Hindsight boundary

Post-entry persistence, retest success, and eventual exit class were used only
to generate this development hypothesis. The proposed confirmation consumes
two new completed daily bars and therefore moves the decision time forward;
execution can begin only at a later legal open. No later bar may be used at the
new entry decision.
